Mai Jiaying Xu

I am a first-year Ph.D. student in Clinical Psychology at the University of Hong Kong. My current project uses targeted memory reactivation (TMR) during sleep as an intervention for fear and anxiety. Broadly, my research examines how childhood adversity shapes mental health, executive function, and developmental trajectories, with a focus on resilience, trauma-related psychopathology, and intervention mechanisms.

I am currently a Research Fellow at the State Key Laboratory of Cognitive Science and Mental Health, Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy of Sciences. My work draws on large-scale datasets, clinical adolescent cohorts, neuroimaging, inflammatory biomarkers, and intervention research to understand how adversity affects cognitive and mental health outcomes, and which protective factors may support recovery and adaptation.

I also collaborate with Harvard's Lab for Youth Mental Health and the Emotion and Psychopathology in Context Lab on meta-analyses of youth interventions and emotion regulation. Previously, I received my M.A. in Experimental Psychology and B.A. in Philosophy and Psychology from Brandeis University.

Research Experience
Clinical Psychology PhD Student, The University of Hong Kong (HKU) (Sept 2026–Present)

Doctoral student in the Clinical Psychology PhD program at HKU, in Prof. Charlene Lam's lab (see the lab page for project details and participation). My first-year project uses targeted memory reactivation (TMR) during sleep as an intervention for fear and anxiety. It combines fear acquisition, generalization, extinction, and return-of-fear paradigms with sleep EEG (cued reactivation during slow-wave sleep), multimodal psychophysiology (skin conductance, heart rate, blood pressure, respiration, and pupillometry), eye tracking, and follow-up fMRI. I also work with transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) and structural and functional MRI, extending a broader interest in developmental psychopathology and in the psychological and neurobiological mechanisms linking early adversity to mental health outcomes across development.
Research Fellow, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Institute of Psychology (June 2025–Present)

Working with Dr. Hang Xu and Dr. Weiwen Wang on the neurobiological and psychological impacts of early adversity. On the human side, I lead multi-method studies on childhood adversity, resilience, and intervention mechanisms across large-scale datasets (UK Biobank), clinical adolescent cohorts, neuroimaging, inflammatory biomarkers, and juvenile intervention trials.

On the animal side, I work with C57BL/6 mice across a full preclinical pipeline:
Electrophysiology & recording: whole-cell patch-clamp recording, in vivo EEG/EMG, and fiber photometry, including craniotomy and stereotaxic surgery, optical fiber and electrode implantation, tethered recording in freely moving animals, and post-surgical care.
Behavioral phenotyping: sucrose preference, open field, elevated plus maze, novel object recognition, three-chamber social interaction, tail suspension, and forced swim tests.
Molecular & histology: gene sequencing and Golgi–Cox staining for dendritic morphology.
